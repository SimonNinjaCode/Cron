## Executive summary

Five cross-source themes stand out in July 2026.

**OAuth Device Code Flow is the defining M365 attack vector of H1 2026.** Forg365, DEBULL, Helix, and CaptiveCrunch all exploit the OAuth 2.0 Device Authorization Flow to bypass MFA entirely. Disabling or restricting this flow via Conditional Access is the single highest-priority control across all tenant types.

**PhaaS industrialization accelerates post-Tycoon2FA.** Microsoft's March 2026 disruption cut Tycoon2FA-associated phishing volume by 92%. But smaller, operationally mature PhaaS platforms (Forg365 at $400/month, ARToken, Kali365) filled the gap. They offer AI-assisted lures, AiTM routing, token handling, and full BEC automation to low-skill actors at commercial scale.

**Physical-to-cloud pivot via infrastructure compromise.** Nation-state and criminal actors are compromising hospitality Wi-Fi infrastructure (DNS poisoning, captive portal AiTM) to redirect corporate travelers to fake M365 authentication portals. This bypasses email gateway, endpoint, and network security controls entirely, and requires no user email interaction.

**Microsoft Teams is the new primary vishing channel.** Weekly Teams-based vishing attempts rose to roughly 10x the mid-2025 baseline by end of Q2 2026, peaking between 14:00-20:00 UTC on weekdays. Post-email social engineering via trusted collaboration platforms is now an established TTP class that demands dedicated detection coverage.

**Detection evasion at the identity plane is a systemic gap.** OAuth Client ID spoofing silently validates stolen credentials without generating sign-in events. ROPC flow abuse generates logs that Conditional Access policies frequently leave uncovered. Identity telemetry gaps in SIEM/UEBA rules are a pervasive risk across M365 tenants.

TTPs: Spoofed OAuth client IDs sent to AADSTS endpoint; AADSTS error-code parsing (e.g., AADSTS50126 vs. AADSTS50034) for credential validation; mass account enumeration without triggering standard sign-in log entries; subsequent targeted password spray or MFA fatigue against confirmed valid credentials. Regions: Global, cross-industry. |
| **Affected Cybersecurity Domain** | Identity & Access / Credential Theft |
| **Risk** | Credential validation at enterprise scale with no observable sign-in event; systemic detection gap in SIEM/UEBA rules relying on AppDisplayName or known application names; mass account lockouts as side-effect collateral damage; enables downstream targeted attacks (password spray, AiTM, BEC) against confirmed valid accounts; tenant-wide identity enumeration at low attribution cost. |

| **Call to Action** | 1. Enable Entra ID Smart Lockout and configure IP-based throttling and named location policies. 3. Monitor AADSTS error codes at scale: Sentinel rule to alert on AADSTS50034 (user not found) and AADSTS50126 (invalid credentials) at volume >500/hour from a single IP or ASN. 4. Enable Entra ID Protection with risk-based Conditional Access policies: block sign-in at Medium+ risk level; require phishing-resistant MFA at Low risk. 5. Disable ROPC/legacy auth flows where not operationally required (reduces the attack surface for client ID spoofing). 6. Threat Hunt (Advanced Hunting, Defender XDR): `AADSignInEventsBeta \| where ErrorCode in (50034, 50126) \| where isempty(ApplicationName) \| summarize Attempts=count() by ClientIP, bin(Timestamp, 5m) \| where Attempts > 50`. |
